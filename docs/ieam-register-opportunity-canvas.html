Opportunity canvas and SWOT · a proposal, not a decision

The IEAM register: a git spine for six taxonomies

The proposal under review: one new repository, ieam-register, holding identities, typed cross-domain relationships, approvals, glossary, mapping matrix, decision records and exceptions for the six IEAM taxonomies, with hierarchy as a parent field in one TOML file per node, dated append-only exports from ARIS, Archi and the enterprise data model, and a standard-library checker in CI. ARIS, Archi and the EDM stay the authors of content; git is authoritative only for what they cannot carry.

The repository, drawn

Three kinds of folder: authoritative records (solid), dated mirrors of tool exports with provenance (dashed), and derived results written at a tag (dotted). Arrows are flows of data; the tools stay the authors of content.

Opportunity canvas

1 · Problems

What hurts today

  • Six taxonomies live in three tools with no shared identity across them.
  • Cross-domain links (requires, realizes, supports, manages) have no owner, rationale, approval or review date anywhere.
  • "Which journeys and products if this module retires?" is answered by hand, each time.
  • No record says which IEAM relationship ARIS carries natively, needs configuration, or cannot carry.
  • Coverage claims arrive without a denominator or a count of exceptions.
2 · Solution ideas

What the register adds

  • One directory per taxonomy, hierarchy in data, never in folder depth.
  • One file per relationship type: kinds, direction, inverse, cardinality per state, owner, evidence class, ARIS and Archi support status, then the links.
  • Approval as a file-state change with approver, date and record; both owners on cross-domain links.
  • Dated exports with manifest and digests; a reconciliation report of adds, updates and deletes.
  • CI refuses dangling links, cycles, duplicates, skipped lifecycle steps; reports coverage with exceptions.
3 · Users and customers

Who touches it

  • The IEAM owner and six domain stewards, approving by pull request.
  • Relationship owners on both sides of a cross-domain link.
  • Architects answering impact and coverage questions.
  • aris2puml and pumllint, which consume the capability inventory and feed trace -f json back.
  • Reviewers and auditors reading the approval trail.
4 · Solutions today

How it is done now

  • ARIS reports and Archi views, read one tool at a time.
  • Spreadsheets of mappings; approvals by email or meeting minute.
  • Capability coverage of process diagrams through footer ids and pumllint trace, on the diagram side only.
  • No reconciliation when an export changes: a vanished object is noticed late or never.
5 · User value

What a steward gets

  • One diffable history of every mapping, with the rationale beside it.
  • A merge that cannot happen while a link dangles or an approval is incomplete.
  • Impact answers as a walk over typed edges, each with its state, owner and evidence.
  • Drafts proposed from exports, approvals never.
6 · User metrics

What to watch

  • Cycle time of a mapping from draft to approved.
  • Share of approved cross-domain links with both owners recorded.
  • Dangling-link and stale-id findings per export reconciliation.
  • Overdue reviews; exceptions open and expiring.
  • Time to answer each of the three decision questions.
7 · Adoption strategy

How it starts

  • Pilot one product and one journey whose processes are already in the aris2puml pilot corpus.
  • Create the six directories and the metamodel on day one; populate one slice.
  • Generate node drafts from the first exports; stewards approve by PR.
  • Expand only after the three questions answer reliably and stewards can keep up.
8 · Business challenges

What the business risks now

  • Retirement and transformation decisions taken on untraceable mappings.
  • Divergent duplicates of the same concept across tools.
  • No audit trail of who approved a cross-domain dependency, or when it was last reviewed.
9 · Business benefits and metrics

What the business gains

  • Decisions with a recorded impact path from offering and journey to module and data.
  • Coverage stated with its denominator and its exceptions, never as a bare percentage.
  • Count of cross-domain mappings with rationale, owner and approval on record.
  • External ids reconciled on every export: fewer silent divergences.
10 · Budget

What it costs

  • Small in code: one repository, standard-library tooling, a CI job.
  • Real in people: steward review time and the validation of ARIS export routes for six domains.
  • Deferred: any Neo4j projection, until a query exceeds what the register's reports answer.

SWOT

SStrengths

  • No second source of truth by construction: node files hold an id, a state, an owner, links and a pointer; a checked name, no copied attributes.
  • Approval is a recorded file state with approver, date and record, four eyes where the lifecycle says so.
  • A dangling link, a cycle, a duplicate id or a skipped lifecycle step blocks the merge; nothing is auto-fixed.
  • Standard-library checker, TOML, dated results and digests: conventions the two repositories already use.
  • Consistent with the standing settlements: no store on pumllint's product path, Loop C outside the seam, typed relations as declared links.
  • An identity spine a later Neo4j projection can read from a tag, with provenance stamped on every node and edge.

WWeaknesses

  • Hand-authored TOML suits dozens of nodes; a thousand-node process taxonomy needs generated drafts and more steward time.
  • A fourth place beside ARIS, Archi and the EDM: one more thing to keep current, even if it holds only links.
  • Approval in git is not approval in ARIS; two governance surfaces until the mapping matrix says which one rules each relationship.
  • Names are checked against the latest export, so a rename in ARIS shows up only at the next reconciliation.
  • Journeys may have to be authored in the register directly if no export carries them, which breaks the "pointer only" rule for one taxonomy.

OOpportunities

  • The three decision questions answered end to end on one real slice: module retirement impact, journey steps without a process, required capabilities without a realising process or a diagram.
  • The first measured test of the footer convention against a capability map, which the ROADMAP has placed in Loop C since 2026-10-29.
  • The mapping matrix doubles as the IEAM's step-one deliverable: every relationship marked native, configured, extension or unsupported, starting at unverified.
  • Reconciliation drafts turn exports into proposals, so stewards review rather than type.
  • A tag-driven projection gives the IEAM's one-way Neo4j publication an identity layer it otherwise has to build twice.

TThreats

  • No ARIS export format, GUID stability or connection name is verified for any of the six domains; a GUID that changes across databases breaks every pointer.
  • ArchiMate 3.2 has no journey element, and the exchange format does not carry EPC, BPMN or journey models.
  • Enterprise data model identifiers: format, stability and export route unknown.
  • Attribute creep: the day a node file copies a field the tool owns, there are two records.
  • The coverage-denominator failure: a refused or unmodelled process reads as a capability gap if the pilot is gated on a percentage.
  • Populating six taxonomies before one slice works, against the IEAM's own second step.

Reading

Worth piloting, on one slice, with the export routes tested first. The strengths are structural and cheap; the threats are almost all about what the tools export, which the pilot is designed to find out. The two things to settle before writing a node file: whether journeys can be pointed at or must be authored, and which of the eight relationships ARIS carries natively.

Two changes to the IEAM text follow from the proposal: the Neo4j projection should read a register tag rather than ARIS directly, and coverage should be reported with its denominator and its exceptions, never used as a gate.
